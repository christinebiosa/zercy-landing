**Short answer:** October gives you summer without the summer crowds. For beaches: Cyprus (81F/27C air, 75F/24C sea), the Canary Islands or the Red Sea. For cities: Andalusia, Malta, Marrakech. For nature: peak fall foliage in New England. For long haul: Egypt at the start of its season. Prices often run 20 to 40 percent below August.

October is the most underrated travel month of the year. School holidays are over, beaches empty out, prices drop. Meanwhile the Mediterranean holds its summer heat for weeks. Travel in October and you get August weather at shoulder-season rates.

For US travelers it is peak leaf-peeping season at home and bargain season in Europe. For UK travelers, October half-term lands right in the sweet spot. Here is where October actually delivers, sorted by travel style, with real numbers instead of vibes.

## Why is October such a strong month to travel?

Three reasons. First, the water: the sea stores summer heat, so the eastern Mediterranean is often warmer in October than in June. Second, the prices: after the summer season ends, hotel rates across the Mediterranean drop 20 to 40 percent, and on Crete sometimes 40 to 50 percent compared with August. Third, the calm: landmarks without lines, beaches without sunbed wars, restaurants with free tables.

Flights get cheaper too. Demand falls after the summer rush and airlines fill seats on price. The fastest way to spot the cheapest October weeks is a flexible-date search on [Aviasales](https://www.aviasales.com/), which shows the whole month at a glance.

The only trade-off: shorter days and the odd rain shower. Plan around that and you lose nothing.

## Beaches: where is the sea still warm in October?

**Cyprus** is Europe's October champion. Daytime highs of 81 to 84F (27 to 29C), sea temperature around 75F (24C), and an average of just 3 to 4 rainy days in the entire month. You can dig into the island's climate data on the [Wikipedia page for the climate of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Cyprus). Beach season here runs into November.

**Crete and Rhodes** sit just behind: sea temperatures of 72 to 75F (22 to 24C), with hotels often 40 to 50 percent cheaper than in August. Most resorts stay open until late October, so you get full infrastructure at half the price.

**Malta** blends beach and culture: 77F (25C) air, 75F (24C) water, plus Valletta and Mdina for the days off the sand.

**The Canary Islands** are the safe bet: 75 to 82F (24 to 28C), water around 73 to 75F (23 to 24C), which is the warmest it gets all year. Southern Tenerife averages only 2 to 3 rainy days. No weather gamble at all.

**The Algarve** is the compromise: air at 73 to 79F (23 to 26C), but the Atlantic stays brisk at about 66F (19C). Albufeira and Lagos in October are ideal for hiking, surfing and long beach walks, less so for hours in the water.

## City breaks: which cities work best in October?

**Andalusia** finally becomes walkable. Seville, Granada and Cordoba roast above 104F (40C) in July; in October daytime temperatures settle around a pleasant 77F (25C). The Alhambra, the Alcazar and the Mezquita without heat haze and without peak-season crowds. Book timed-entry tickets ahead on [Tiqets](https://www.tiqets.com/), because the Alhambra still sells out even in October.

**Marrakech** hits its high season in October for good reason: the brutal summer heat is gone and the souks and riads are at their best. The full month-by-month breakdown is in our guide to the [best time to visit Morocco](/en/blog/best-time-to-visit-morocco/).

**Rome, Lisbon and Athens** all work better in October than in midsummer: 68 to 77F (20 to 25C), soft light, shorter lines. For photographers, the low October light in southern Europe is a reason to go on its own.

The simple math of an October city break: you walk 20,000 steps a day without melting.

## Nature and fall colors: New England and Japan

If the beach is not your thing, October is the biggest color show of the year.

**New England** is the classic. Peak foliage moves north to south: Vermont and the White Mountains light up in late September, and by mid-October the color wave reaches southern New Hampshire, the Berkshires and coastal Maine. A road trip from Boston through Vermont to Maine almost always catches peak color somewhere in October.

**Japan** needs fine-tuning: the koyo season starts in October in Hokkaido and the mountains, for example around Lake Chuzenji near Nikko in mid to late October. Kyoto does not turn until the second half of November. For a pure October trip, Nikko and the north are the right call.

**The Alps** are Europe's shortcut: golden larch forests in South Tyrol and the Engadine from mid-October, with crisp air and empty trails. More ideas are in our guide to the [best autumn foliage destinations](/en/blog/best-autumn-foliage-destinations/).

## Long haul: where does the season start in October?

**Egypt and the Red Sea** answer the guaranteed-warmth question. Hurghada and Marsa Alam sit around 86F (30C) with sea temperatures of 79 to 81F (26 to 27C) in October, and virtually zero rain. For divers and snorkelers this is the start of the best season: great visibility, summer heat gone.

**Oman** also opens its season in October: the extreme summer heat fades, and the months for desert, wadis and Muscat begin.

**Be careful with Southeast Asia:** Thailand, Vietnam and Bali are still partly in monsoon or transition mode in October. Check regions carefully before booking. The reliable long-haul warmth in October sits to the west: the Red Sea, Morocco, the Canaries.

The price angle on long haul: October sits before the Christmas peak. Flights to the Red Sea typically cost far less in October than in December, for identical weather.

## Which October destination fits you?

The quick decision list by travel style:

- **You want maximum beach warmth:** Cyprus or the Red Sea. Water at 75 to 81F, minimal rain risk.
- **You want a beach with a short flight:** Crete or the Canaries. Up to 40 to 50 percent below August prices.
- **You want culture and old towns:** Andalusia or Marrakech. Finally cool enough to walk all day.
- **You want nature and color:** New England for the big trip, the Alps for a long weekend.
- **You want diving or snorkeling:** the Red Sea, right at the start of its best visibility.
- **You are traveling with kids over half-term:** the Canary Islands. No weather risk, short flights, no jet lag from Europe.

Timing rule: for half-term and holiday weeks, book flights 2 to 3 months out; outside those weeks, 4 to 6 weeks is usually enough. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where is the warmest place in Europe in October?

Cyprus and the Canary Islands. Cyprus reaches 81 to 84F (27 to 29C) by day with a 75F (24C) sea and only 3 to 4 rainy days. The Canaries run 75 to 82F (24 to 28C), and the ocean there hits its annual peak of 73 to 75F (23 to 24C) in October.

### How much cheaper is traveling in October?

Mediterranean hotels typically cost 20 to 40 percent less in October than in August, and on Crete the gap can reach 40 to 50 percent. Flights also drop once summer demand fades. The exception: half-term and fall-break weeks, when family routes spike again.

### Which long-haul destination works best in October?

The Red Sea. Hurghada and Marsa Alam offer around 86F (30C) air, 79 to 81F (26 to 27C) water and almost no rain. It is also the start of the best diving visibility, and October fares run well below Christmas-season prices.

### When should you book an October trip?

For half-term or fall-break weeks: 2 to 3 months ahead, so by July or August at the latest. Outside holiday weeks, 4 to 6 weeks of lead time is usually enough, because shoulder-season hotels have spare capacity and fill rooms on price.
