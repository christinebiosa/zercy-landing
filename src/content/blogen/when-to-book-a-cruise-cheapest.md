**Short answer:** For most travelers, booking early wins: 12 to 18 months before sailing you get the widest cabin choice and solid fares. Wave season, January through March, delivers the fattest perks like onboard credit and discounted drink packages. Last minute only pays off if you are flexible and live near a home port. The real insider move: repositioning cruises in October, November and April, often 40 to 70 percent cheaper per night.

Cruise pricing works differently from airfare. A line needs every ship full, because an empty cabin spends nothing on board. That is why the industry runs predictable discount windows every year, and knowing them can cut hundreds of dollars off the exact same itinerary. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_ship), more than 300 cruise ships operate worldwide with over 660,000 berths. Your leverage.

This guide compares the three big strategies head to head, with real numbers and a decision framework at the end. If you have never sailed before, start with our [cruise beginners guide](/en/blog/cruise-beginners-guide/) so the basics are covered.

## What is wave season and how much do you actually save?

Wave season is the industry name for January through March, when cruise lines run their most aggressive promotions of the year. It is when Americans book the bulk of their cruises, and Carnival, Royal Caribbean and Norwegian compete hard for those deposits. The deals apply to sailings stretching deep into the following year.

What is inside the deals varies by line. During wave season 2026, promotions included up to 40 percent off drink packages, internet and shore excursions, plus free fares for third and fourth guests in a cabin. Royal Caribbean advertised up to 1,000 dollars off cruise fares. Cunard offered up to 600 dollars in onboard credit per stateroom. Oceania ran up to 30 percent off across its fleet with a reduced 50 percent deposit.

Key point: wave season rarely slashes the base fare the hardest. The total value rises instead. Onboard credit, cabin upgrades and bundled packages do the heavy lifting. The drink package especially deserves a calculator before you celebrate, and we ran the honest math in [is the cruise drink package worth it?](/en/blog/cruise-drink-package-worth-it/).

## When does booking early pay off the most?

The rule of thumb: book 12 to 18 months before departure. That is when lines open new itineraries, often at introductory fares, and when you get first pick of cabin location, deck and dining time. Midship cabins on middle decks, well-placed balconies and the few solo cabins always sell first.

Early booking wins in three situations. First, school-holiday sailings: summer break, Christmas, spring break. Those departures fill early and almost never get cheaper later. Second, new ships and high-demand routes like Alaska in summer. Third, when you want a specific cabin category, say a family cabin or a suite.

A bonus many people miss: if the fare drops before your final payment date, many lines will match the new price or add onboard credit if you ask. Nobody volunteers this.

## How risky is booking last minute?

Last minute in cruising means 30 to 90 days before sailing. Final payment is typically due around 90 days out, which is when undecided bookers cancel and the line finally sees how full the ship really is. Leftover cabins then get discounted, and cuts of 20 to 40 percent off brochure rates are realistic in that window.

But there are three serious catches.

First, cabin location. What remains is what nobody wanted: insides under the nightclub, cabins next to the anchor or behind a lifeboat. Second, getting to the port. The cruise fare drops, but last-minute flights to the departure port get expensive. Saving 300 dollars on the cabin means nothing if the flight costs 350 dollars more. Check the flight the moment you spot a cruise deal, for example on [Aviasales](https://www.aviasales.com/), before you commit. Our guide on [when to book flights](/en/blog/when-to-book-flights/) explains how that market moves. Third, choice. You no longer pick the date, the route or the ship. The clearance rack does.

Last minute works best for people who live within driving distance of a home port like Miami, Port Canaveral, Galveston or Seattle, have flexible dates and do not care where they sleep.

## The insider play: repositioning cruises from about 100 dollars per night

Twice a year, entire fleets switch regions. In October and November, ships leave the Mediterranean for the Caribbean. In April, they sail back. Rather than crossing empty, lines sell these one-way transatlantic passages as repositioning cruises. Demand for 13 to 16 nights with long stretches of sea days is limited, so fares often land 40 to 70 percent below comparable round trips.

Real-world numbers from fall 2026: balcony cabins on mainstream lines at roughly 100 to 160 dollars per person per night, and inside cabins from about 98 dollars per day. Departures typically leave from Barcelona, Lisbon, Rome or Southampton toward Florida and the Caribbean.

The deal has conditions. You need time, you need to enjoy sea days, and you need two one-way flights. Add extra days at the start or end port and this becomes the best-value long vacation in travel. Ideas for that are in [how to extend cruise port cities](/en/blog/cruise-extend-port-cities/).

## Which off-season windows push prices down further?

Beyond when you book, when you sail matters. Two windows stand out.

**Caribbean in September and October.** This is the heart of hurricane season, and that is exactly why fares often run 20 to 40 percent below winter rates, sometimes more. The risk is smaller than it sounds: if a storm crosses the route, ships usually reroute rather than cancel. You trade port predictability for a much better price.

**Mediterranean in November.** After the fall break crowds leave, temperatures in Lisbon, Málaga and Palermo stay mild and fares drop well below summer levels. For city lovers it is arguably the nicer season, because the ports are not overrun.

In both windows, remember: shore excursions sold on board stay expensive even when the cabin was cheap. Booking your own port days often cuts that bill in half, and you can grab attraction tickets in advance on [Tiqets](https://www.tiqets.com/). ## Early booking, wave season or last minute: which one fits you?

Here is the decision framework, short and honest:

- **Book early (12 to 18 months out)** if you are tied to school holidays, want a specific cabin or travel as a family. Best selection, predictable pricing, and you can often claim a price drop before final payment.
- **Book during wave season (January to March)** if perks matter to you. Onboard credit, discounted drink packages and free third and fourth guests are often worth more than a small fare cut. Use it to book sailings later in the year or the year after.
- **Book last minute (30 to 90 days out)** if you are flexible, live near a home port and do not care about cabin location. Always price the flight at the same time.
- **Book a repositioning cruise (October, November or April)** if you have time and want the lowest cost per night in cruising. Nothing else comes close.

One mistake ruins all four strategies: comparing cabin prices only. Always total cabin plus flights plus gratuities plus drinks plus excursions. Only then do you see which deal wins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When is a cruise cheapest to take?

Per night, repositioning cruises in October, November and April are the cheapest option, often 40 to 70 percent below regular routes. For classic round trips, the best fares show up in the off-season, like the Caribbean in September and October or the Mediterranean in November.

### How far in advance should you book a cruise?

For school holidays, new ships and high-demand routes, 12 to 18 months ahead gives you the best cabins and stable fares. For off-season dates, 3 to 6 months usually works. True bargains appear 30 to 90 days before sailing, when leftover cabins go on clearance.

### What is wave season in cruising?

Wave season is the industry discount period from January through March, when cruise lines run their biggest promotions of the year. Typical offers include onboard credit, up to 40 percent off drink packages and excursions, cabin upgrades and free fares for third and fourth guests.

### Why do last-minute cruise deals often cost more than expected?

Because the cabin savings get eaten by the journey to the port. Last-minute flights to departure cities are expensive, and the remaining cabins are usually the worst locations on the ship. Last minute really pays off only if you can drive to the port and stay flexible.
